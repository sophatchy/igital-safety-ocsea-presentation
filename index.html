01 / Understand the issue

What is OCSEA?

Online Child Sexual Exploitation and Abuse — online tools may be used to contact, manipulate, threaten, exploit, or share sexual material involving a child.

Contact

Grooming & manipulation

Building trust, asking for secrecy, moving to private chats, or testing boundaries to exploit a child.

Coercion

Sextortion & threats

Using images, fabricated images, or threats to demand money, more images, sexual acts, or silence.

Distribution

Image-based abuse

Creating, possessing, or sharing sexual images of children, including AI-manipulated material, without a child’s safety or consent.

Remember: a child is never to blame for being manipulated or threatened. Responsibility belongs to the person causing the harm.

03 / Recognise the pattern

How manipulation can escalate

Not every case follows the same path, but these warning signs can help adults intervene early.

1

Contact

A stranger or acquaintance approaches through a game, group, social platform, or messaging app.

2

Trust & secrecy

Excessive attention, gifts, flattery, requests to hide the chat, or pressure to move to a private channel.

3

Boundary testing

Requests for personal details, intimate images, live video, or increasingly sexual conversations.

4

Threats or control

Blackmail, demands, impersonation, threats to share content, or attempts to isolate the child.

Warning signs are not proof by themselves. Listen calmly, ask open questions, and focus on the child’s safety rather than blame or punishment.

04 / Technology & misconceptions

AI, private chats & the Dark Web

Generative AI

Fabricated imagery can still cause real harm

AI may manipulate a real child’s photo into sexualised content or create fake material used for humiliation, coercion, or sextortion. A fake image is not “harmless” simply because it is synthetic.

Encryption

Privacy tools are not the problem by themselves

End-to-end encryption protects legitimate private communication, but can make harmful content harder for platforms to detect. Avoid claiming encryption alone proves wrongdoing or makes a service unsafe.

Private messaging

Secret chats and disappearing messages

Disappearing messages do not guarantee that a recipient cannot save, photograph, or share the content. Report features and evidence availability differ by service.

Dark Web

A network, not a synonym for all crime

Some hidden services are abused for illegal activity, including child sexual abuse material. Do not explore, download, or redistribute illegal content. Report it safely through appropriate channels.

Practical lesson: safety depends on behaviour, consent, safeguards, reporting routes, and trusted support—not a single app setting or technology label.
05 / Scenario discussion

A sextortion scenario

A fictional learning scenario. This is not a real person or a reported case.

What happens

“Send money or I will post it.”

A teenager receives a threat from an account that claims to possess a private image. The account demands payment and warns the teenager not to tell anyone. The image may be real, altered, or fabricated.

What helps

Safety over negotiation

Tell a trusted adult; do not pay or send more material; stop engaging; preserve safe evidence of the account and threats; report the account and seek appropriate child-protection or law-enforcement support.

Ask the audience: What is the first supportive sentence you would say? What information can be recorded without copying or forwarding the intimate image itself?

06 / Respond safely

Four steps: stop, save, report, support

01 · STOP

Do not pay, threaten back, send more images, or agree to meet. Block contact when safe to do so.

02 · SAVE

Record usernames, profile links, dates, messages, and threats where safe. Do not download, duplicate, or forward sexual images of children.

03 · REPORT

Use the platform’s reporting tools and seek help from trusted child-protection professionals or appropriate local authorities.

04 · SUPPORT

Believe the child, stay calm, protect their privacy, and avoid blame. Arrange ongoing emotional and practical support.

Adults and organisations: limit access to evidence, avoid circulating details, follow safeguarding procedures, and do not promise absolute secrecy if a child may be at risk.

Prevention that works best as a conversation

Set privacy controls together; keep accounts and devices updated; use strong unique passwords and multifactor authentication; discuss image-sharing pressure and AI manipulation; make sure children know whom they can tell without fear of punishment.

07 / Close & references

Make safety a shared responsibility.

Children should not have to solve exploitation alone. Families, educators, platforms, civil society, and public institutions each have a role.

Notice

Recognise grooming, coercion, threats, non-consensual image-sharing, and AI-enabled abuse.

Respond

Listen without blame, preserve safe evidence, report through appropriate channels, and support the child.

Prevent

Teach digital literacy, build trustworthy reporting pathways, and strengthen platform and institutional safeguards.
